1. SMART MONEY CONCEPTS (SMC)
   ID: mod_smc | Weight: 1.82 | File: SMCStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   The methodology used by institutional traders — banks, hedge funds, prop desks.
   Tracks where large capital enters and exits the market through structural patterns.
   Highest-weighted module because it directly models institutional behavior.

CORE CONCEPTS:

Order Blocks:
   - Demand OB: Last bearish candle before a bullish impulse move upward.
     These are areas where institutions placed buy orders. When price returns,
     they will defend the level (more buying).
   - Supply OB: Last bullish candle before a bearish impulse move downward.
     Institutions placed sell orders here. Return = more selling.
   - Validity: OB is valid until price closes through it.
   - Implementation: Scan last 50 bars, find impulse moves (> 2× ATR body),
     identify the final opposite-direction candle before impulse.

Fair Value Gaps (FVG / Imbalance):
   - 3-bar pattern: If bar[i-2].high < bar[i].low → bullish FVG (gap up, unfilled).
   - Bullish FVG: Zone between bar[i-2].high and bar[i].low. Price fills, bounces.
   - Bearish FVG: bar[i-2].low > bar[i].high. Zone between those two prices.
   - Markets are fractal — gaps get filled eventually. Entry on fill, continuation expected.

Break of Structure (BOS):
   - Higher high in established downtrend = bullish BOS. Trend may be reversing.
   - Lower low in established uptrend = bearish BOS. Trend may be reversing.
   - First BOS = Change of Character (CHoCH) — earliest reversal warning.
   - Subsequent BOS confirm new direction.

Entry Logic:
   - Ideal setup: Price returns to OB that is also within an FVG.
   - Confluence: OB + FVG alignment = highest probability entry zone.
   - Target: Next major swing high/low on opposite side.
   - Invalidation: Close through the OB (not just a wick).

WHY HIGH WEIGHT:
   Explains HOW institutional money moves. When you see a spike down with
   immediate recovery, that's a liquidity grab into institutional demand.
   SMC provides the structural framework all other methods build on.

2. TJR PRICE ACTION
   ID: mod_tjr | Weight: 1.71 | File: TJRStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   A proprietary price action system built on institutional candle analysis.
   Focuses on identifying where large orders entered the market through candle
   size, position, and subsequent price behavior.

CORE CONCEPTS:

Institutional Candles:
   - Large body candle (body > 2× recent ATR) that gaps away from prior range.
   - Represents a single large order or coordinated institutional entry.
   - The 50% level (midpoint) of the candle body = optimal entry zone on retest.

Liquidity Grabs:
   - Wick extends beyond recent swing high/low, then closes back inside range.
   - Classic stop hunt. Institutions need liquidity (stop orders) to fill their orders.
   - After the grab, price moves opposite direction rapidly.

Key Levels:
   - Highs and lows of institutional candles serve as magnetic support/resistance.
   - These levels hold because the original order placer will defend their position.

Entry Strategy:
   - Wait for retest of institutional candle midpoint (50% retracement).
   - Enter in direction of the original impulse.
   - Stop below the candle low (demand) or above candle high (supply).
   - Target: next institutional candle on opposite side.

3. WYCKOFF METHOD
   ID: mod_wyckoff | Weight: 1.60 | File: WyckoffStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Developed by Richard Wyckoff (1930s). Studies the relationship between price
   movement, volume, and time to determine the actions of the "Composite Man"
   (the collective force of large operators). One of the oldest and most proven methods.

ACCUMULATION PHASES:
   PS  — Preliminary Support: First attempt to stop decline, volume increases.
   SC  — Selling Climax: Panic selling, highest volume, wide spread down bar, closes off lows.
   AR  — Automatic Rally: Short covering + new buyers, prices bounce.
   ST  — Secondary Test: Price returns to SC area on lower volume. Key test.
   Spring — False breakdown below support. Traps shorts. Volume spike. Close above support.
   SOS — Sign of Strength: Strong rally on high volume after Spring.
   LPS — Last Point of Support: Small pullback on low volume after SOS. Best entry.
   BU  — Back Up: Return to broken resistance (now support). Confirms accumulation.

DISTRIBUTION PHASES:
   PSY — Preliminary Supply: First selling pressure after uptrend.
   BC  — Buying Climax: Euphoric buying, highest volume, wide up bar.
   AR  — Automatic Reaction: Sharp sell-off as longs liquidate.
   ST  — Secondary Test: Rally back to BC area on lower volume.
   UTAD — Upthrust After Distribution: False breakout above resistance. Traps longs.
   LPSY — Last Point of Supply: Weak rally on low volume. Best short entry.
   SOW — Sign of Weakness: Breakdown on high volume.

EFFORT VS RESULT:
   High volume + small price move = absorption (opposing force is strong).
   High volume + large price move = genuine trend (go with it).
   Low volume + large price move = easy movement (trend has no opposition, go with it but exit fast).
   Low volume + small price move = weakness drying up (anticipate directional move).

WHY IT WORKS:
   All markets go through accumulation and distribution. The Composite Man
   (large money) must accumulate before they can mark up. Must distribute
   before they can mark down. The sequence is logical and repeating.

4. SUPPLY AND DEMAND ZONES
   ID: mod_supply_demand | Weight: 1.55 | File: SupplyDemandZoneStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Zone-based support/resistance methodology. Finds areas where an imbalance between
   buyers and sellers previously caused a rapid price move, indicating unfilled orders remain.

ZONE DETECTION:
   Base bar: Tight consolidation bar (range < 1.2× ATR). Price going sideways = orders accumulating.
   Explosive move: Next 1-2 bars move > 2× ATR from the base. Orders triggered.

   Demand Zone (long): Base bar followed by explosive UP move.
   Supply Zone (short): Base bar followed by explosive DOWN move.

   Zone boundaries: High and low of the base bar(s).

FRESHNESS SCORING:
   Fresh zone (0 retests): Confidence 0.76. All unfilled orders still present.
   1 retest:               Confidence 0.62. Some orders filled, zone weakened.
   2+ retests:             Confidence 0.45. Zone likely exhausted. Avoid.

   Logic: Every time price enters the zone, some orders get filled.
   A zone visited 3+ times has very few orders left. It will break.

RETEST DETECTION:
   Bar low < zone high AND bar high > zone low = bar overlaps zone = retest counted.

ENTRY TIMING:
   Price entering demand zone for 1st time = highest probability long.
   Price entering supply zone for 1st time = highest probability short.
   Set stop beyond zone boundary + small buffer.
   Target: Next supply/demand zone on opposite side.

5. VOLUME PROFILE
   ID: mod_volume_profile | Weight: 1.54 | File: VolumeProfileStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Distributes traded volume across price levels rather than time.
   Shows WHERE the market actually traded, not just when.

KEY LEVELS:
   POC (Point of Control): Price level with highest volume. Strongest magnet.
   Value Area High (VAH): Upper boundary of 70% volume zone.
   Value Area Low (VAL): Lower boundary of 70% volume zone.

High Volume Nodes (HVN): Areas of heavy trading = strong S/R (price spends time here).
Low Volume Nodes (LVN): Areas of light trading = air pockets (price moves fast through).

TRADING RULES:
   Price at VAL = buyers defend value → LONG bias.
   Price at VAH = sellers defend upper value → SHORT bias.
   Price breaking VAH with volume = expanding value area = trend.
   Price returning to POC after breakout = retest of strongest level.
   LVN below price = easy run down if broken. LVN above = easy run up.

IMPLEMENTATION:
   50-bucket histogram over 100-200 bars. Each bucket = price range.
   Volume accumulated per bucket. POC = max bucket. Value area = 70% of total volume.

6. LIQUIDITY HUNT
   ID: mod_liquidity_hunt | Weight: 1.48 | File: LiquidityHuntStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Markets move to where stop orders cluster. Retail traders place stops at obvious
   levels (swing highs/lows, round numbers, equal highs/lows). Institutions sweep
   these levels to get filled, then reverse direction.

PATTERN: BULLISH LIQUIDITY SWEEP
   1. Identify swing low with 2-bar confirmation each side.
   2. Price wicks below swing low (bar.low < swingLow × 0.998).
   3. Price closes above swing low (bar.close > swingLow).
   4. = Stop orders triggered below swing low. Sellers trapped. Buyers filled.
   5. Entry: Close of sweep candle. Target: Prior swing high.
   Confidence scales with wick size relative to ATR.

PATTERN: BEARISH LIQUIDITY SWEEP
   1. Identify swing high.
   2. Wick above swing high, close below.
   3. = Stop orders triggered above swing high. Buyers trapped. Sellers filled.
   4. Entry: Close of sweep candle. Target: Prior swing low.

EQUAL HIGHS / EQUAL LOWS:
   Two or more swing highs within 0.2% of each other = equal highs.
   Retail traders place buy stops above both highs. Obvious target for institutions.
   Equal highs swept then close below = strong SHORT signal.
   Equal lows swept then close above = strong LONG signal.

SESSION EXTREMES:
   Prior session high/low often swept at open. Same logic applies.
   Price takes out yesterday's high then reverses = stop hunt complete.

WHY IT WORKS:
   Institutions need liquidity (opposing orders) to fill their large positions.
   Stop orders = limit orders for the opposing side. Perfect fill for large orders.

7. VOLUME SPREAD ANALYSIS (VSA)
   ID: mod_vsa | Weight: 1.45 | File: VSAStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Tom Williams / Richard Wyckoff methodology. Analyzes the relationship between
   spread (price range), close position, and volume to read smart money's actions.
   Volume shows HOW MUCH effort. Spread shows the RESULT of that effort.

SIGNALS:

Selling Climax (BULLISH reversal):
   Wide spread + ultra-high volume (> 3× avg) + close in LOWER half of bar.
   = Smart money absorbing panic selling from retail. Supply exhausted. Bull signal.

Stopping Volume (BULLISH reversal):
   Wide down bar + ultra-high volume + close ABOVE midpoint.
   = Buyers stepping in to absorb selling. Down move losing energy. Bull signal.

Buying Climax (BEARISH):
   Wide spread + ultra-high volume + close in UPPER half of bar.
   = Smart money distributing into euphoric retail buying. Demand exhausted.

No Demand (BEARISH continuation):
   Narrow spread + low volume (< 0.7× avg) + up close.
   = No institutional buying behind rally. Move is weak. Expect failure.

No Supply (BULLISH continuation):
   Narrow spread + low volume + down close.
   = No selling pressure behind decline. Move is weak. Expect reversal.

Effort vs Result Mismatch:
   High volume + tiny price move = absorption (trapped side is losing).
   Low volume + sustained directional move = easy movement (no opposition).

VOLUME THRESHOLDS:
   Low: < avgVol × 0.7
   High: > avgVol × 2.0
   Ultra-high: > avgVol × 3.0
   avgVol calculated from last 20 bars.

8. MARKET PROFILE
   ID: mod_market_profile | Weight: 1.42 | File: MarketProfileStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   J. Peter Steidlmayer (CME, 1980s). Organizes market activity by price and time
   rather than just time. Shows accepted value (where most trading occurs) vs
   rejected price (extremes that will be revisited).

CONCEPTS:

Point of Control (POC):
   Price level with maximum volume over session/lookback period.
   Strongest reference level. Price gravitates back to POC.

Value Area (70% Rule):
   Range containing 70% of total session volume.
   VAH (top of value area) = where sellers found acceptance.
   VAL (bottom) = where buyers found acceptance.

Poor Highs / Poor Lows:
   Two or more consecutive bars at the same high = auction was stopped artificially.
   = Unfinished business. Market will return to this extreme.
   Same logic for consecutive equal lows.

TRADING LOGIC:
   Below VAL: LONG — price is below accepted value. Will revert to value.
   Above VAH: SHORT — price is above accepted value. Will revert.
   At POC: Both sides compete. Wait for break with volume.
   Poor High + at VAH: Strong SHORT setup (rejection + unfinished business above).
   Poor Low + at VAL: Strong LONG setup.

IMPLEMENTATION:
   50-bucket price histogram over last 100 bars.
   Accumulate volume per bucket. POC = max. Expand from POC ± until 70% volume captured.

9. VWAP & ANCHORED VWAP
   ID: mod_vwap | Weight: 1.38 | File: VWAPStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Volume-Weighted Average Price. The institutional benchmark. Algorithms, index funds,
   and large players use VWAP as execution benchmark. Price relationship to VWAP
   tells you who is in control — buyers or sellers.

CALCULATION:
   TP = (high + low + close) / 3  (typical price)
   VWAP = Σ(TP × volume) / Σ(volume)  (running cumulative)
   σ = standard deviation of (TP - VWAP) weighted by volume
   Bands: VWAP ± 1σ, ± 2σ

SIGNALS:

VWAP Reclaim (BULLISH):
   Price was below VWAP, now closes above = buyers taking control.
   Confidence: 0.65. Entry on reclaim close. Stop: back below VWAP.

VWAP Rejection (BEARISH):
   Price was above VWAP, now closes below = sellers taking control.
   Confidence: 0.65. Entry on rejection close.

+2σ (Upper Band) — SHORT:
   Price reached extreme. Statistically expensive relative to volume-weighted average.
   Mean reversion expected. Confidence: 0.68. Stop: close above +2σ.

-2σ (Lower Band) — LONG:
   Price reached extreme cheap. Mean reversion expected.
   Confidence: 0.68. Stop: close below -2σ.

VWAP Pullback (BULLISH continuation):
   Price above VWAP, pulls back to VWAP from above = buy the dip at institutional benchmark.
   Confidence: 0.65. Common professional entry technique.

ANCHORED VWAP:
   VWAP anchored from significant swing point (major high/low, earnings date, etc.)
   Acts as S/R level that accounts for volume at each price since anchor.

WHAT IT IS:

KEY LEVELS:
   EMA 9:  Short-term momentum (used by day traders, swing traders)

   EMA 50: Medium-term trend reference (institutional interest)

SIGNALS:

EMA Stack (Bearish): 9 < 21 < 50 < 200. All aligned downward. Strong downtrend.

EMA Divergence: EMAs spreading apart = trend accelerating, stay in trade.

Price below EMA 200 = bearish bias. Sell rallies. Avoid longs.
Price above EMA 200 = bullish bias. Buy dips. Avoid shorts.

EMA 50 as pullback level: In trending market, pull back to EMA 50 = continuation entry.

11. CLASSICAL TECHNICAL ANALYSIS PATTERNS
    ID: mod_classical_ta | Weight: 1.31 | File: ClassicalPatternsStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Traditional chart pattern recognition. These patterns work because they represent
   recurring human psychology — greed, fear, hope — that creates predictable price structures.

REVERSAL PATTERNS:

Head & Shoulders (BEARISH reversal):
   Three peaks. Left shoulder + higher head + right shoulder (lower than head).
   Neckline: drawn through the two troughs. Break below neckline = entry.
   Target: Head height projected down from neckline.
   Most reliable reversal pattern in technical analysis.

Inverse H&S (BULLISH reversal): Mirror image. Break above neckline = entry.

Double Top (BEARISH):
   Two equal highs within 1-3%. Neckline = trough between them.
   Break below neckline with volume = confirmed. Target = pattern height down.

Double Bottom (BULLISH): Two equal lows. Break above neckline.

Triple Top / Bottom: Like double but with three tests. Even stronger confirmation.

CONTINUATION PATTERNS:

Bull Flag: Sharp rally (flagpole) → parallel channel pullback → breakout upward.
Bear Flag: Sharp decline → parallel channel rally → breakout downward.
Pennant: Sharp move → symmetrical triangle consolidation → continuation.

Ascending Triangle: Flat top + rising lows. Buyers more aggressive each time. Bullish breakout.
Descending Triangle: Flat bottom + falling highs. Sellers. Bearish breakdown.
Symmetrical Triangle: Converging lines. Neutral until breakout direction determined.

Rising Wedge: Both lines rising but converging. Bearish (rally losing steam).
Falling Wedge: Both lines falling, converging. Bullish (decline losing momentum).

Cup & Handle:
   Rounded bottom (cup) + small consolidation (handle) + breakout = bullish.
   Most reliable continuation pattern in stocks.

MEASURED MOVES:
   All patterns have measured move targets = pattern height projected from breakout.
   Example: Double top pattern is 5% tall → target is 5% below neckline.

12. DIVERGENCE ANALYSIS
    ID: mod_divergence | Weight: 1.28 | File: DivergenceStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Compares price swing highs/lows to oscillator (RSI) swing highs/lows.
   When they disagree, momentum is weakening → reversal or continuation signal.
   One of the highest-accuracy signals in technical analysis when used with confluence.

INDICATOR: RSI 14-period.

REGULAR DIVERGENCE (reversal signals):

Regular Bullish Divergence:
   Price makes LOWER LOW. RSI makes HIGHER LOW.
   = Price falling but momentum strengthening. Sellers losing steam. BUY signal.
   Only valid when RSI < 45 (actually in oversold territory).
   Confidence: 0.68.

Regular Bearish Divergence:
   Price makes HIGHER HIGH. RSI makes LOWER HIGH.
   = Price rising but momentum weakening. Buyers losing steam. SELL signal.
   Only valid when RSI > 55 (actually in overbought territory).
   Confidence: 0.68.

HIDDEN DIVERGENCE (continuation signals):

Hidden Bullish Divergence:
   Price makes HIGHER LOW (uptrend). RSI makes LOWER LOW.
   = Trend is intact but RSI pulled back more. Buyers still in control. BUY continuation.
   Confidence: 0.62.

Hidden Bearish Divergence:
   Price makes LOWER HIGH (downtrend). RSI makes HIGHER HIGH.
   = Trend intact but RSI rallied more. Sellers in control. SELL continuation.
   Confidence: 0.62.

IMPLEMENTATION:
   Calculate full RSI array. Find swing highs/lows in price and RSI using
   10-bar lookback window (findMaxIdx / findMinIdx helpers).
   Compare last two swings. If they diverge = signal.

13. MEAN REVERSION
    ID: mod_mean_reversion | Weight: 1.22 | File: MeanReversionStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Markets oscillate around a mean. When price moves statistically too far from
   average, probability favors return. Best used in ranging/low-volatility markets.

INDICATORS:
   Z-score = (current price - 20-bar SMA) / 20-bar standard deviation
   Bollinger Bands = SMA ± 2σ
   ADX = Average Directional Index (14-period). Measures trend strength.

REGIME FILTER (critical):
   ADX < 25 = ranging market → mean reversion valid. TRADE IT.
   ADX > 25 = trending market → mean reversion fails. DO NOT TRADE.
   This filter is what separates profitable mean reversion from disaster.

SIGNALS:
   Z < -2.0 AND ADX < 25 = LONG. Price statistically cheap. Revert to mean.
   Z > +2.0 AND ADX < 25 = SHORT. Price statistically expensive.

   Confidence = min(|z-score| / 3, 0.85) × ADX discount factor.
   Z-score of -3.0 = max confidence (extreme oversold). Z-score of -2.0 = lower conf.

TARGETS:
   Long target: SMA (mean). Maybe SMA + 0.5σ if strong move.
   Short target: SMA.
   Stop: 3σ from mean (price becomes MORE extreme before reverting = stop out).

BOLLINGER BANDS:
   Upper band (SMA + 2σ): SHORT entry zone.
   Lower band (SMA - 2σ): LONG entry zone.
   Band width: Narrow = low volatility squeeze. Expansion imminent.
   Band walk: Price hugging upper/lower band = trend, not mean reversion environment.

14. MOMENTUM STRATEGY
    ID: mod_momentum | Weight: 1.18 | File: MomentumStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Trend-following using oscillator signals and rate of change.
   Momentum strategies work because trends persist — winners keep winning,
   losers keep losing (momentum factor in academic finance).

INDICATORS:
   RSI 14: Relative Strength Index. > 60 = bullish momentum. < 40 = bearish.
   MACD: 12-26 EMA difference + 9 EMA signal line. Crossovers = momentum shifts.
   ROC: Rate of Change. Measures acceleration of price movement.
   Stochastic %K/%D: Fast oscillator for shorter-term momentum.

SIGNALS:
   RSI > 60 + MACD bullish crossover + ROC positive = strong LONG momentum.
   RSI < 40 + MACD bearish crossover + ROC negative = strong SHORT momentum.

   Momentum divergence from price (via RSI/MACD) = potential reversal (see Divergence module).

MOMENTUM ANOMALY:
   Academic evidence: stocks/assets that performed best in past 3-12 months
   continue to outperform next 3-12 months. This is the momentum factor.
   Underlying cause: underreaction to information. Markets update slowly.

15. OPENING RANGE BREAKOUT (ORB)
    ID: mod_orb | Weight: 1.15 | File: OpeningRangeStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   The opening range (first N bars of a session) often sets the tone for the day.
   Breakouts from this range have high follow-through probability because large
   institutional orders often execute at open and establish direction.

OPENING RANGE CALCULATION:
   1m TF:  First 30 bars (30-minute range)
   5m TF:  First 6 bars (30-minute range)
   15m TF: First 4 bars (60-minute range)
   30m TF: First 3 bars (90-minute range)
   1H TF:  First 2 bars (first 2 hours)
   Higher: Not meaningful (range = first bar)

   ORH = highest high in opening range
   ORL = lowest low in opening range

VALIDITY FILTER:
   Range must be 0.3% to 3% of price. Too tight = fake. Too wide = already moved.
   Risk:reward must be minimum 1.8:1.

ORB LONG: Close above ORH + volume > 1.2× average.
   Entry: Close of breakout bar OR retest of ORH from above.
   Target: ORH + (ORH - ORL) × 1.5 (measured move).
   Stop: ORL.

ORB SHORT: Close below ORL + volume confirmation.
   Target: ORL - (ORH - ORL) × 1.5.
   Stop: ORH.

FAILED ORB (FADE):
   Price breaks out → fades back inside range within 1-3 bars.
   = Breakout was a stop hunt. Reverse the breakout.
   High-probability reversal if volume was low on breakout.

16. VOLATILITY STRATEGY
    ID: mod_volatility | Weight: 1.10 | File: VolatilityStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Identifies volatility regimes and trades the expansion/contraction cycle.
   Low volatility always precedes high volatility. The squeeze is the setup.

INDICATORS:
   ATR (Average True Range): Measures current volatility in price terms.
   Bollinger Bandwidth: (Upper - Lower) / SMA. Measures band width relative to price.
   ATR percentile: Current ATR vs 252-period range.

SIGNALS:

Volatility Squeeze + Breakout:
   Bollinger Bands narrow to multi-week lows (bandwidth < 20th percentile).
   = Energy coiling. Breakout imminent. Prepare for directional trade.
   When bands expand = enter in direction of expansion.

High ATR = Wide Stops:
   Context signal. When ATR is elevated, all strategies should widen stops 1.5×.
   Risk flags generated in AnalysisEngine when volatility > 0.7.

Low ATR = Size Down:
   Volatility squeeze could break either direction. Reduce position size.
   Risk flag when volatility < 0.1.

Band Touch Entries:
   In ranging market: price touches lower band = LONG. Touches upper band = SHORT.
   Must confirm with volume and other signals — band touches alone fail in trends.

17. DONCHIAN CHANNEL BREAKOUT (TURTLE TRADING)
    ID: mod_donchian | Weight: 1.08 | File: DonchianBreakoutStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   The Turtle Trading System, designed by Richard Dennis and William Eckhardt (1983).
   Two mechanical breakout systems. Among the most tested trading systems in history.
   Proven profitable over decades across markets.

SYSTEM 1 (Fast System):
   Entry: Price closes above 20-bar highest high → LONG.
          Price closes below 20-bar lowest low → SHORT.
   Exit: Price closes below 10-bar lowest low (for longs).
         Price closes above 10-bar highest high (for shorts).
   Stop: 2× ATR from entry.
   Confidence: 0.62.

SYSTEM 2 (Slow System — more reliable):
   Entry: Price closes above 55-bar highest high → LONG.
          Price closes below 55-bar lowest low → SHORT.
   Exit: Price closes below 20-bar lowest low (for longs).
   Stop: 2× ATR from entry.
   Confidence: 0.72.

CHANNEL COMPRESSION (Squeeze Detection):
   Current channel range (high - low) < 55% of 200-bar average range.
   = Market contracting. Breakout is coming. Higher confidence on next signal.

WHY IT WORKS:
   Trends are real and persistent. If price makes a new 55-bar high, something
   fundamental has changed. Major trends (commodity supercycles, equity bull markets,
   currency trends) are caught by this system. Misses all the noise.

18. INTERMARKET ANALYSIS
    ID: mod_intermarket | Weight: 0.95 | File: IntermarketStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   John Murphy methodology. Studies correlations between asset classes to confirm
   or deny signals from price action on the primary instrument.

KEY RELATIONSHIPS:
   DXY (Dollar) ↑ → Commodities (gold, oil) ↓ (inverse correlation).
   DXY ↑ → Emerging market equities ↓ (USD debt burden).
   Bonds ↑ (yields ↓) → Equities ↑ (risk-on, lower discount rate).
   Gold ↑ → Risk-off environment. Watch equity sell-offs.
   Oil ↑ → Energy stocks ↑, consumer spending pressure ↑.
   VIX ↑ → Equities ↓ (fear rising).

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
   True intermarket analysis requires live data for multiple instruments simultaneously.
   Current implementation uses price action proxies from the primary symbol's bar data
   to estimate correlational signals. Lower weight reflects this limitation.

WHEN USEFUL:
   Confirming a LONG equity signal when bonds are rising = solid confluence.
   Fading a LONG equity signal when dollar is surging = caution flag.
   Best used as contextual filter, not primary signal generator.

21. ELLIOTT WAVE THEORY
    ID: mod_elliott | Weight: 0.71 | File: ElliottWaveStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Ralph Nelson Elliott (1930s). Markets move in predictable wave patterns driven by
   collective investor psychology. 5-wave impulse + 3-wave correction, repeating fractally.

IMPULSE WAVES (1-2-3-4-5):
   Wave 1: Initial move. Often small, unrecognized.
   Wave 2: Retracement of wave 1. Never exceeds start of wave 1. Often 61.8% retrace.
   Wave 3: Largest, strongest wave. Never shortest. Often 161.8% of wave 1. Most profitable.
   Wave 4: Consolidation. Never enters wave 1 territory. Often 38.2% retrace of wave 3.
   Wave 5: Final push. Often equals wave 1 in size. Divergence common.

CORRECTIVE WAVES (A-B-C):
   Wave A: First leg down.
   Wave B: Rally. Can be sharp. Often confuses people into thinking trend resumed.
   Wave C: Final decline. Often equals wave A. Usually sharp and brutal.

FIBONACCI RATIOS:
   Wave 2 = 61.8% of wave 1
   Wave 3 = 161.8% of wave 1 (target)
   Wave 4 = 38.2% of wave 3
   Wave 5 = 61.8%–100% of wave 1

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
   Automated wave counting is notoriously difficult. Wave counts are subjective —
   different analysts count differently. The 0.71 weight reflects this. Used as
   target projection tool more than entry signal. Fibonacci ratios are reliable;
   wave count labeling is not.

22. ORDER FLOW ANALYSIS
    ID: mod_orderflow | Weight: 0.68 | File: OrderFlowStrategy.ts
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT IS:
   Level 2 / tape reading / footprint chart analysis. Studies the actual flow of
   buy and sell orders at each price level. The most granular view of who is
   winning the battle between buyers and sellers.

TRUE ORDER FLOW (requires tick data):
   Bid/ask volume at each price tick.
   Delta = Ask volume - Bid volume. Positive = buyers aggressive. Negative = sellers.
   Imbalances: More buy volume than sell at a level = buyers absorbing sellers.
   Absorption: Large sell orders absorbed by buyers without price falling = bullish.
   Exhaustion: Price makes new high but delta turns negative = buyers running out.

OHLCV PROXY IMPLEMENTATION:
   Since tick data is unavailable, delta is estimated:
   If bar close > bar open = buying pressure dominant.
   If bar close < bar open = selling pressure dominant.
   Volume-weighted delta over recent bars.
   Absorption proxy: high volume + small spread.

   Accuracy is limited without real tick data. Hence lowest weight (0.68).

WHEN MOST ACCURATE:
   Near key levels (S/R, OBs, zones). Absorption at support = strong LONG signal.
   On breakouts: high volume + expanding spread = genuine break.
                 high volume + failing to advance = fake break.

FUTURE IMPROVEMENT:
   Integrate with broker API for real order book data. Or use tick data provider.
   With real footprint data, this module would be among highest-weighted.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PREDICTION GENERATION METHODOLOGY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

After analysis completes, 40 future bars are generated:

1. ATR from last 14 bars = measure of recent volatility.
2. Direction bias from consensus (LONG=+1, SHORT=-1, NEUTRAL=0).
3. Trend component per bar: price × trendPct × decayFactor
   - trendPct = (compositeScore / 10) × 0.015 × bias  (max 1.5%/bar at score=10)
   - decayFactor = e^(-i × 0.04)  — trend fades over 40 bars (uncertainty grows)
4. Noise component: ATR × (0.7 + i × 0.02) × seededRandom
   - Noise increases with horizon. Bar 1 = 70% ATR noise. Bar 40 = 150% ATR noise.
   - Seeded LCG ensures same analysis produces same prediction shape.
5. Candle generated: open=prev_close, move=trend+noise, wick=ATR×random(0.15-0.50)

Visual rendering:
   - Blue color scheme: up #1565c0, down #0d47a1, wick #42a5f5
   - Dashed vertical line at boundary: "NOW" left, "FORECAST →" right
   - Blue semi-transparent shade on forecast half of chart
   - Forecast ON/OFF toggle button (top-right of chart, z-20)

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CONFLUENCE SCORING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. All 22 modules run in parallel.
2. Signals grouped: bullish vs bearish.
3. Winning direction = more signals OR higher weighted confidence sum on tie.
   weightedSum = Σ(confidence × weight) for each signal.
4. Minimum 3 modules must agree AND direction ≠ NEUTRAL → canPublish = true.
5. compositeScore = (avgConfidence × 0.6 + (agreedCount/22) × 0.4) × 10
   Example: 15 modules agree, avgConfidence = 0.72
   = (0.72 × 0.6 + (15/22) × 0.4) × 10
   = (0.432 + 0.273) × 10 = 7.05/10
6. Score = 0 when canPublish = false (less than 3 agree or NEUTRAL wins).

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
ADAPTIVE LEARNING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Every prediction saved to SQLite. Every 30 minutes, OutcomeChecker evaluates:

Scoring:
   +5 direction correct
   +3 target 1 hit
   +3 target 2 hit
   +1 entry zone respected
   -3 invalidation hit (stopped out)
   -3 direction wrong (cancels all positive scoring)
   Range: -3 to +11

Weight adjustment per agreeing module:
   reward = score / 110
   newWeight = clamp(currentWeight + reward, 0.1, 2.0)

A module that calls LONG and price goes straight to T2 gets +11/110 = +0.10 reward.
A module that calls LONG and gets stopped out immediately gets -3/110 = -0.027 penalty.
Over time, accurate modules drift toward 2.0, weak modules drift toward 0.1.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STRATEGY SELECTION PHILOSOPHY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Highest weighted (1.5+): Institutional methods that directly track smart money behavior.
   SMC, TJR, Wyckoff, Supply/Demand, Liquidity Hunt.
   These work because they identify WHERE institutions placed orders.

Mid-tier (1.0-1.5): Proven technical methods with strong academic/empirical backing.
   VSA, Market Profile, VWAP, MAs, Classical Patterns, Divergence, Mean Reversion, Momentum.
   These work because they measure real price/volume dynamics.

Support tier (0.7-1.0): Context and confirmation, not primary signals.
   ORB, Volatility, Donchian — mechanical systems with edge but not always active.
   Intermarket, Sentiment, Seasonality — macro context and probability bias.

Lower weight (< 0.75): Useful but approximate, noisy, or data-limited.
   Elliott Wave (subjective), Order Flow (requires tick data for accuracy).

The weighting philosophy: All 22 modules should vote, but institutional methods
should count more. Consensus of weaker methods still matters. A 15-7 vote with
strong weighted sum = high confidence. A 12-10 vote with weak weighted sum = low confidence.
